Data center capacity · capital & partnerships

Overview of US data centers under construction and recently delivered, and the capital and partners behind them.

Due to the significant capital requirements, existing power constraints, and execution risks, increasing AI capacity depends on a variety of strong relationships throughout the data center life cycle. This map plots known US data centers and identifies the structures used to add capacity and the counterparties behind it: developers, financial institutions, lenders, colocation providers, and power partners. Meta is shown in blue; select any site to open its capital stack, deal highlights, and key stakeholders.

Each dossier shows the announced capital, the deal terms (equity/debt split, spread, GMP, guarantees, lease terms), the parties by role, and the capital stack drawn as a one-line — equity at the top, contractual credit support at the bottom.

Fields that are not public are marked as such rather than estimated.

Key Stakeholders

The data center ecosystem spans financial institutions, developers, contractors and power partners. Scaling capacity requires a deep understanding not only of each player's role, but of their requirements, return objectives and desired outcomes.

What this is

A working map of how large-scale US data center capacity is being financed and owned, built to compare structures rather than count buildings. Assembled from public sources — not an investment recommendation.

Confidence marking

Filed — company release or securities filing. Announced — named-party press release. Reported — press citing unnamed sources; terms may move.

Known gaps

General contractors, GMP terms, per-site capacity (MW), and fund LP identities are disclosed inconsistently. Those fields are left blank rather than inferred. Marker size reflects announced capacity (MW/GW) where available; portfolio-level platforms plot at HQ.

Sources

Company releases, investor relations material, securities filings, and trade press. Per-deal source links appear in each dossier.
